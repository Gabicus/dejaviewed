The AI Capex Stack — reading it like a credit analyst

Insight class: Emergent Capability · Difficulty: Medium · Quality: 3/5 Connected entries: 8 · Tools: Anthropic, Canva, Claude, Codex, DeepSeek, Grokbot, OpenAI, Qwen, Unsloth

Thesis

An ex-Morgan Stanley structurer explains that CoreWeave-style GPU loans have a thin tail: loan, contract and GPU useful life are nearly the same length, so the bull case rests on hourly rental prices after contracts end. Other saves price that number from the demand side: open weights flipped to about 80% of token volume in 12 weeks, DeepSeek serves near-frontier agents at $0.28 per million tokens, a 27B model runs on one gaming GPU, and a $200 subscription buys about $7,000 of API usage. Token deflation is a direct input to post-contract GPU rental rates, and nobody in the catalog connects them.

Why it matters

You can track the one variable the neocloud debt depends on with public price pages and a scheduled agent. Physical infrastructure with real order books, like chillers, is a different bet from GPU-hour rental, and this lens separates them.

Action sketch — how to implement this

Schedule an agent to log daily H100/B200 hourly rental prices from public marketplaces and the cheapest per-million-token price for three model tiers on OpenRouter into one table.

Pull neocloud debt and contract maturities from public filings and plot rental price against the period when large contracts roll off.

Write a monthly one-page note: is GPU-hour price falling faster than token volume is rising? Paper only, no positions.

falsifier: if GPU-hour rental prices hold flat or rise while token prices fall, usage growth (Jevons) is absorbing the deflation and the tail-risk thesis is wrong.
