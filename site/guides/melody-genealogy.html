Melody Genealogy — Crate-Digging the Public Domain with Local Search

Insight class: Emergent Capability · Difficulty: Hard · Quality: 4/5 Connected entries: 12 · Tools: Banjo, Radiooooo, Unreal Engine

In short: Build a local, fully offline melody-genealogy engine that does by machine what Chance McCoy does by ear: cluster public-domain recordings into tune families and map how melodies travelled across centuries and languages.

Thesis

Chance McCoy traces Shady Grove back to Child Ballad #81 and Sally in the Garden to a 1929 Brunswick 78 — by ear, one tune at a time. Sample diggers do the same hunt for producer gold, also by hand. Local multimodal search like SentrySearch already finds moments in hours of video from a plain-language query. Point that pattern at Lomax, Prelinger and 78-rpm archives with audio embeddings, and you get a tune family tree plus cleared public-domain samples, each with provenance.

Why it matters

Folk players, music teachers and producers get a searchable map of how melodies travelled across centuries, languages and continents. Producers get a legally clean sample source with a story attached — the opposite of crate-digging your way into a clearance fight.

3 · Step-by-step prompts

Step 1 — Gather recordings and clear rights twice

Goal: Build a 200 to 500 recording corpus where composition and recording rights are each verified.

Pull 200 to 500 recordings from public-domain sources: Library of Congress collections (including the Lomax field recordings and the Nimrod Workman lineage @dimestoreradio shows), Internet Archive 78-rpm collections, and Prelinger film audio. Include the anchors @chancemccoymusic names: 'Shady Grove' versions (Doc Watson, Jean Ritchie), 'Matty Groves', 'Sally in the Garden' (Crockett's Kentucky Mountaineers, Brunswick 291), and 'Hog-Eye Man' tunes. For each recording, verify rights twice: the composition (often public domain if pre-1929 in the US) and the specific recording (a 1929 recording's rights can differ). Record both in manifest.csv with the rights source, and set cleared=y only when both are clear. Fetch locally and keep audio on the machine. Record the duration and recording year of each item, since the lineage graph orients edges by date. Keep a cleared-count so you know how many recordings are actually usable in the sample pack, separate from the full corpus.

Expect: corpus/ with the audio, and manifest.csv of 200 to 500 rows carrying composition rights, recording rights, year, source URL and a cleared flag.

Audit before moving on: Sample 20 rows and confirm both the composition and the recording rights against the source. If a recording's rights are unclear, set cleared=n and exclude it from the sample pack. Fail if any row conflates composition rights with recording rights, or has an empty rights source.

Step 2 — Extract transposition-invariant melody and audio embeddings

Goal: Represent each recording as both an interval contour and an audio embedding, stored locally.

For each recording, extract the monophonic melody with CREPE or pYIN (via librosa). Convert the pitch track to a sequence of intervals (semitone steps between successive notes) so the representation is transposition-invariant, which matters because the same tune appears in different keys and modal tunings, like the D-Dorian 'Shady Grove'. Separately, compute an audio embedding with an open CLAP audio encoder for timbral and overall similarity. Store both representations in ChromaDB, following the SentrySearch (ssrajadh/sentrysearch) fully-local layout: one collection for melody-interval vectors, one for audio embeddings, with metadata linking back to the recording and timestamp. Keep everything offline. Handle polyphonic or noisy 78s by flagging low-confidence melody extractions rather than trusting them. Store the raw pitch track alongside the quantized intervals for debugging, but index only the intervals. Note the sample rate and any resampling, since 78-rpm transfers vary and pitch extraction is sensitive to speed errors.

Expect: index/ with a ChromaDB store holding interval-contour and CLAP vectors per recording, plus a confidence flag on each melody extraction.

Audit before moving on: Verify transposition invariance: transpose a test recording by 3 semitones, re-extract, and confirm its interval sequence is nearly unchanged. If it changes a lot, fix the interval quantization. Flag melody extractions below a confidence threshold. Fail if raw pitch (not intervals) was stored.

Step 3 — Build ground truth and score the clustering

Goal: Test whether known tune families actually cluster, before drawing any tree.

Build a labelled ground-truth set: assign known family labels from the Child ballad numbers and the Roud Folk Song Index to the recordings where the literature establishes them. At minimum: 'Shady Grove' and 'Matty Groves' as Child #81, the 'Hog-Eye Man' group, and a few unrelated control tunes that should not cluster together. Cluster both representations (interval contours and audio embeddings) with HDBSCAN or agglomerative clustering. Score the clustering against the ground truth: precision, recall, and adjusted Rand index. Specifically check: do 'Shady Grove' and 'Matty Groves' land in the same cluster? Do the controls stay apart? State the falsifier up front: if known families do not cluster above chance, the embedding search is noise and the hand method stays the only method.

Expect: eval/ with the ground-truth labels, cluster assignments, and a score table (precision, recall, adjusted Rand index) with the known-family checks.

Audit before moving on: Compare against a random-label baseline to define 'above chance'. If the interval and audio representations disagree, report both and do not cherry-pick the flattering one. Fail if the Shady Grove / Matty Groves check is not explicitly reported, pass or fail.

Step 4 — Build the lineage graph with cited edges

Goal: Map the tune family tree, but only if the evaluation passed.

Proceed only if step 3 showed known families clustering above chance. If it did not, skip to the report and record the falsifier result. Build a lineage graph in NetworkX. Nodes are recordings or tune variants. Edges connect variants that are melodically close (by interval-contour distance), with the edge weight being the similarity. Every edge must carry a timestamped link back to the source recording that supports it, in the SentrySearch spirit of returning exact timestamps. Orient edges by date where the recording year is known, so the graph reads as travel over time: Renaissance ballad to Appalachian standard, 1929 Brunswick to later field recordings. Add the cross-language edges as test cases: the Mandarin 'Winner Takes It All' and Russian Ievan Polkka should connect to their source melodies despite different lyrics. Render a force-directed web view.

Expect: lineage/ with the NetworkX graph (GraphML or JSON) and an interactive force-graph view, every edge citing a timestamped source.

Audit before moving on: Pick 10 edges at random and confirm each links to a real source recording and timestamp. Confirm the Mandarin and Russian versions attach to the correct melody, not to each other by language. Fail if any edge has no source citation, or if the graph was built despite a failed eval.

Step 5 — Package one cleared sample pack

Goal: Turn one validated cluster into a legally clean sample pack with provenance.

Pick one cluster that clustered cleanly and whose recordings are all cleared=y in both composition and recording rights. Cut usable sample segments (loops, one-shots, vocal phrases) from those recordings. Write a provenance sheet per sample: the source recording, its year, the composition and recording rights basis, and the lineage position (which tune family, which known relatives). This is the opposite of crate-digging into a clearance fight, as @baauer's 'Sleepyhead' sample story warns. Assemble the pack with the samples, the provenance sheet, and a short written family history. Prepare a free release and define how you will measure producer pickup (downloads, uses, tags). Keep the exact in and out points for each sample so cuts are reproducible. Note the tempo and key of each sample, because producers filter on those and it makes the pack more useful.

Expect: pack/ with sample files, a per-sample provenance sheet, a family-history note, and a pickup-measurement plan.

Audit before moving on: Confirm every sample in the pack traces to a cleared=y recording, with both rights cleared. If any sample's recording rights are unclear, pull it. Fail if the pack ships without a provenance sheet, or includes a recording cleared only at the composition level.

Step 6 — Test the Platonic Representation claim

Goal: Check whether cross-source embedding similarity is real signal, honestly.

The dive leans on @lizthedeveloper's framing of the Platonic Representation Hypothesis: different models' embedding spaces converge, so melodic similarity might be findable across sources. Test it rather than assume it. Take the interval-contour similarity and the CLAP audio similarity for the same pairs of recordings. Correlate them. If two independent representations agree on which tunes are related, that is evidence the similarity is real structure, not an artifact of one model. Report the correlation and where the two disagree, since disagreements are the interesting cases: same melody, different timbre (a cross-language cover), or similar timbre, different melody (a false friend). Do not overclaim: a positive correlation supports the method, it does not prove any specific lineage edge.

Expect: a correlation analysis between the melodic and audio similarity spaces, with the agreement and disagreement cases listed.

Audit before moving on: If the two representations correlate weakly, say so plainly and lean on the interval contours (the musically meaningful one) for lineage, not the audio embedding. Fail if a lineage edge rests only on audio-timbre similarity with no melodic support.

Step 7 — Validate against oracles and write the gap report

Goal: Deliver the clustering verdict, the falsifier result and the ranked gaps.

Run the oracle pass: (a) Known families cluster above chance: the precision, recall and adjusted Rand index against the Child/Roud ground truth, and the explicit 'Shady Grove' / 'Matty Groves' check. (b) Transposition invariance of the melody representation, verified by the transpose test. (c) Every lineage edge cites a timestamped source recording. (d) Every sample in the pack is cleared at both the composition and recording level. (e) The falsifier: if known families do not beat chance, say plainly the embedding search is noise and the hand method stays the only method. Write report.md with the sections Corpus, Index, Evaluation, Lineage, Sample Pack, Platonic-Representation Check and Gaps. State whether the engine actually finds tune families. Rank the gaps by impact.

Expect: report.md, a five-row oracle table, and a ranked gap list.

Audit before moving on: Any lineage claim without a cited source is removed. Any 'above chance' claim without the random baseline is downgraded. Confirm the pack's rights are cleared at both levels. Fail if the falsifier section is missing or the Shady Grove check is absent.

5 · What proves it right

Known tune families cluster above chance against a labelled Child/Roud ground-truth set, scored by precision, recall and adjusted Rand index, with the 'Shady Grove' / 'Matty Groves' (Child #81) check reported explicitly.

Transposition invariance: transposing a recording and re-extracting leaves its interval contour nearly unchanged.

Every lineage edge links to a timestamped source recording, SentrySearch-style, with no uncited edges.

Every sample in the released pack is cleared separately at the composition and recording level, with a provenance sheet.

Agreement between the interval-contour and CLAP audio similarity spaces supports that found similarities are real structure, not one model's artifact.

6 · Failure modes

Rights conflation. Tell: a 1929 recording called public domain because the tune is old. Fix: verify composition and recording separately and clear both before sampling.

Key-dependent matching. Tell: the same tune in two keys does not cluster. Fix: compare transposition-invariant interval sequences, not raw pitch.

Pattern in noise. Tell: a pretty graph with no ground-truth score. Fix: score against known families versus a random baseline before drawing the tree.

Uncited edges. Tell: the lineage graph asserts a link with no source. Fix: require a timestamped source recording on every edge or drop it.

Timbre false friends. Tell: two tracks cluster because they share a scratchy 78 sound, not a melody. Fix: lead with interval contours and treat audio embedding as secondary.

Overclaiming telepathy. Tell: the Platonic Representation idea used to assert lineage. Fix: test the correlation and claim only method support, not specific edges.
