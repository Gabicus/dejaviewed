Mocap as Reward — teaching cheap robots to move like you

Insight class: Emergent Capability · Difficulty: Experimental · Quality: 3/5 Connected entries: 7 · Tools: Blender, JupyterLab, Microduck, Unity, Unreal Engine, XInputReader, ppf-contact-solver

Thesis

The Microduck RL demo shows the classic failure: a loose reward function makes the robot 'stand' in a way nobody intended. The catalog also holds the fix, scattered across collections. FreeMoCap turns a webcam into markerless motion capture, HY-Motion produces motion from text on robot skeletons, and Blender plus a GPU contact solver can rehearse physical contact. Use captured or generated motion as an imitation reward on a cheap open robot. The reward-design trial and error becomes 'show it what you mean'.

Why it matters

Hobby robotics is gated by reward engineering more than by hardware. A webcam reference clip gives an individual the motion-prior pipeline that only labs used to have, for small expressive robots, animatronics and installations.

Action sketch — how to implement this

Reproduce the Microduck standing run in its sim with 4,096 envs; confirm the reward hack.

Record a 10-second webcam clip of a 'bob and look around' motion with FreeMoCap (or generate one with HY-Motion), retarget to Microduck's joints, and add a pose-tracking imitation term to the reward.

Compare policies: reward-only vs reward + imitation, scored by a blind viewer on 'looks intentional' and by joint-tracking error; deploy the winner to hardware if you have it.

Falsifier: if retargeting human motion to a duck-scale robot body produces infeasible joint targets that the policy can't track better than the plain reward, the motion prior is the wrong shape for small robots.
