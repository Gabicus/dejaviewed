Step 2 — Solve the IPO game

Goal: Write and solve the day-one IPO game in extensive form.

Using claims.csv, model the SpaceX listing in pygambit. The players are underwriters, who set price and allocation; insiders under a 180-day lockup; institutional allocants, who flip or hold; retail, who buys at the open or waits; and holdco holders. Information sets matter: retail cannot see allocation demand, and underwriters can. Take payoffs from data. Use Ritter's average first-day pop and three-year underperformance for large IPOs, and the observed FB, UBER and RIVN paths. Solve by backward induction. Cross-check the normal-form reduction with nashpy. Name the commitment devices that make up the actual 'trap': lockup expiry, the greenshoe, directed-share programs and index inclusion lags. Run a sensitivity table that perturbs each payoff by plus and minus 50%, and report which perturbation flips retail's best response.

Expect: games.py containing Game A, a sourced payoff table, the solver output, a text tree and the sensitivity table.

Audit before moving on: If the equilibrium flips inside the plus or minus 50% band, label it fragile. Every payoff must trace to claims.csv or carry an 'assumed' label. Nashpy and Gambit must agree. Treat any disagreement as a bug.

Step 3 — Event study plus capped paper arm

Step 4 — Solve the failed strategy

Goal: Show with a solved game why the expired-API-domain squat fails, or where it does not.

Model @crimistickman's squat. Step 5 — AI race game and stability

Goal: Model the Hinton-to-OpenAI arc as a repeated commitment game and test whether its equilibrium is stable.

Model two or three labs choosing Race or Restrain each period, with talent moving toward the best mix of compute, pay and mission. This is the dynamic @buysiders describes from AlexNet onward. Step 6 — Math anchor: the probabilistic method

Goal: Prove the 3Blue1Brown 10-point puzzle and tie it to mixed strategies.

Prove that any 10 points in the plane can be covered by disjoint unit discs. Use a hexagonal unit-disc packing with density pi/(2 sqrt 3), about 0.9069. Translate it by a vector drawn uniformly over a fundamental domain. Each point is covered with probability 0.9069, so by linearity of expectation about 9.069 of the 10 points are covered. Since 9.069 > 9, some translate covers all 10. Verify this numerically in sim.py. Draw 10,000 random and adversarially clustered 10-point sets and grid-search translates until all 10 points are covered. Compute the largest n for which n x 0.9069 > n - 1. Then write one paragraph on why this averaging argument is the logic behind mixed strategies: a random choice whose expectation guarantees that a good pure choice exists.

Expect: proof.md, sim.py with a 100% covering rate, the n bound (n up to 10), and the connecting paragraph.

Audit before moving on: Compute the density to 6 decimals. If any sampled set fails, refine the grid before calling it a counterexample. A failure means a bug, because the proof is sound.
