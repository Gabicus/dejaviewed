One Dancer, a Thousand Bodies — Ensemble Choreography from Herd Physics

Insight class: Emergent Capability · Difficulty: Experimental · Quality: 3/5 Connected entries: 9 · Tools: After Effects, Arc, Blender, Claude, FFmpeg, JupyterLab, Microduck, Python, React, Unity, Unreal Engine, XInputReader, ppf-contact-solver

Thesis

Crystal Pite's ensemble pieces make dozens of dancers move like one organism, and that takes a company and months of rehearsal. Now webcam mocap captures one dancer, text-to-motion models spin out variations, and the quant collection's Ising herding model supplies the missing rule: one temperature knob that moves a crowd from noise to sudden collective turns. That last step is a market model used here as a choreographic score.

Why it matters

A solo dancer or animator can compose crowd choreography with real intention — not a boids screensaver — and render it with production cloth physics. It's a new kind of score: the choreographer sets coupling and temperature, and the dancer's signature motion spreads through the crowd.

Action sketch — how to implement this

Capture 5 minutes of one dancer with FreeMoCap (2–3 webcams), retarget to a Blender rig, and generate 20 variants of each phrase with HY-Motion prompts

Have Claude write a Blender script: 200–1,000 instances on a lattice, each with an Ising spin (which phrase or facing to adopt) updated by neighbor coupling, plus boids spacing; expose temperature as a driver on a gamepad axis

Render a 90-second piece where you drop temperature through the critical point live, then add PPF cloth to a 50-body subset for the hero shot

Falsifier: if viewers describe it as a screensaver or particle effect rather than a crowd of people with intention, the herd score isn't carrying meaning
