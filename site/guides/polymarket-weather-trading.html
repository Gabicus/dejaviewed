One of the most-repeated Polymarket folk stories of 2025 is "a student built a six-figure portfolio trading weather contracts from his dorm with a DIY sensor rig." The sensor-rig version of the story is mostly myth — the actual edge is more boring, more reproducible, and more interesting: multi-model ensemble forecasting against a retail-priced order book. The weather station is optional flavor. The ensemble is the alpha.

This guide covers the strategy, the real infrastructure the top traders use, why weather was the perfect market to build this on, and what's replicable.

1. What it is

Polymarket runs hundreds of daily/weekly weather contracts: "High temperature in NYC on March 15 between 42°F and 45°F," "Will it rain tomorrow in London," "Max temp in Buenos Aires above X." Each bucketed range is a separate binary contract. Dozens of top wallets have made $20k–$2M+ trading these markets, and several of the top 10 Polymarket wallets by PnL are weather-only.

Named traders:

  • gopfan2 — $2M+ net, almost entirely from weather. Known for simple price rules (buy YES under $0.15, buy NO over $0.45, ~$1 max risk per position) and extreme volume.
  • neobrother — $20k+ from "temperature laddering" — buying low-cost bets across many temperature buckets to cover a wider range than the market implied.
  • Hans323 — $1.11M on a single $92k bet on London's weather (~8% implied probability).

The edge isn't knowing the weather better than NOAA. The edge is knowing it better than the order book, which is a much lower bar.

2. How it works — the actual strategy

Core thesis

Weather forecasts from professional models (GFS, ECMWF, UKMO, NWS, ICON, GEM, HRRR) are extremely good 12-72 hours out. Trained 4-model ensembles hit ~70-75% accuracy on daily-high temperature buckets and daily precipitation. Polymarket contracts on those same events trade on retail sentiment, copy trading, and stale prices — often for hours after a new model run has materially shifted the forecast.

That gap is the edge. You're arbitraging retail prediction market prices against publicly available, free, scientifically rigorous physical models.

The ensemble

No single model is reliable. The top bot (WeatherBot) uses a 4-model ensemble weighted as:

Model Weight
ECMWF (European) 35%
GFS (US global) 25%
UKMO (UK Met Office) 20%
NWS (US, bias-corrected MOS) 20%

With outlier penalization — if one model disagrees sharply, its weight gets cut. Degen Doppler runs a much larger 13-model ensemble (Tomorrow.io, Pirate, Visual Crossing, etc.) and dynamically reweights by recent accuracy per region and time-of-day.

For each Polymarket bucket [T_lo, T_hi] on day D, you compute:

P_ensemble(T_lo ≤ T_actual ≤ T_hi) = Σ_m w_m · P_m(T_lo ≤ T ≤ T_hi)

where P_m is derived from the ensemble-member spread (or from raw ensemble members in the case of GFS's 31-member GEFS).

The trade

  • If P_ensemble > ask_price + edge_threshold → buy YES.
  • If P_ensemble < bid_price − edge_threshold → buy NO (equivalent to selling YES).
  • Size with Kelly or fractional Kelly on P_ensemble vs market price.
  • Exit either at resolution or when price converges to fair value (rolling the capital to the next mispricing).

Why this works

  1. Polymarket weather markets are liquid but not efficient. There's enough volume to trade, but retail traders drive prices with gut feel, not NWS MOS output.
  2. Model runs update every 6 hours. When GFS 12z drops and shows NYC 5°F warmer than the 06z run, the probability stack for the daily high buckets shifts instantly. Polymarket lags this by 30-90 minutes. That lag is a free option.
  3. Bucketed contracts enable laddering. Instead of one bet, you buy a probability-weighted basket across 3-5 adjacent buckets around the ensemble mode, which dramatically reduces variance.
  4. Weather is a mean-reverting, well-understood physical process. Unlike political or sports markets, there are no hidden catalysts, no insider info, no interpretation risk.

3. Key mechanics

Data sources (all free):

  • NOAA NOMADS — GFS grib2 files, refreshed 4x/day
  • ECMWF Open Data — since Jan 2022, free up to 10 days out
  • UKMO & ICON via open APIs
  • NWS API for MOS station forecasts (this is the one closest to Polymarket's resolution source)
  • Visual Crossing, Open-Meteo — aggregated APIs if you don't want to parse grib2

Resolution source matters. Polymarket weather markets resolve against specific weather station reports (usually the "official" NWS observation for the city). Your model needs to predict that station, not the city average. This is where NWS MOS (station-specific bias-corrected output) dominates raw GFS.

Position lifecycle:

  1. Pull new model runs every 6 hours (00z, 06z, 12z, 18z UTC).
  2. Recompute ensemble PDF for each open market's resolution variable.
  3. Diff against current Polymarket CLOB best-bid/best-ask.
  4. Size positions with Kelly or fixed fraction. Most successful bots cap per-position risk at $1-$100.
  5. Post limit orders (not market orders — slippage kills the edge on thin markets).
  6. On the next model run, recompute and either scale up, reverse, or hold.

Fee structure quirk: Polymarket fees scale by min(price, 1−price), so extreme-price contracts (near $0.01 or $0.99) have the lowest effective fees. This is also what enables the 99c limit arbitrage strategy (see polymarket-99c-limit-arbitrage.md).

4. The infrastructure — what's actually running

Three public references exist:

Full-stack reference implementation. Trades both Kalshi (KXHIGH series) and Polymarket. Uses GFS 31-member ensemble (GEFS) forecasts plus — notably — BTC 5-minute microstructure as an auxiliary signal. Kelly sizing, signal calibration, React dashboard. Claimed max profit ~$1.8k. Best starting point for a working codebase.

hcharper/polyBot-Weather

Simpler single-model bot. Educational.

WeatherBot (weatherbot.finance) & Degen Doppler

Commercial tools. Closed source. WeatherBot publishes its 4-model weighting scheme as marketing; Degen Doppler publishes its 13-model list. You can replicate both from public APIs.

The "dorm / weather station" angle

The viral story about a student using a rooftop weather station for edge doesn't hold up: a single-point sensor can't beat a continental-scale numerical weather model for even 12-hour forecasts, because the model already ingests tens of thousands of station observations. Where a local sensor helps is arbitraging the last-hour resolution print — detecting whether the NWS station has already recorded the high for the day, which Polymarket prices don't always reflect until it's official. That's a nowcasting edge, not a forecasting edge, and it requires co-location near (or API access to) the specific resolution station. Short range, narrow edge, real but overstated.

5. How to replicate

Minimal viable weather trader (1 weekend):

pip install polymarket-py open-meteo-requests requests numpy
  1. Use Open-Meteo's free ensemble API (GFS + ECMWF + ICON + GEM) — skip grib2 parsing entirely. Returns raw ensemble members.
  2. Pull current Polymarket weather markets via the CLOB REST API.
  3. For each market, compute P_ensemble(bucket) as the fraction of ensemble members falling in the bucket.
  4. Buy any bucket where P_ensemble − ask > 0.10 with $1-10 per position.
  5. Hold to resolution.

This is enough to learn the edge. Scaling to $10k+ requires real-time order management, slippage control, and a watchdog that can cut positions on model disagreement.

Data path to productionize:

  • Open-Meteo → GFS/ECMWF/ICON ensemble members
  • NWS API → station MOS forecasts
  • NOAA NOMADS → raw GEFS 31-member if you want more resolution
  • ECMWF Open Data → raw IFS ensemble (2 runs/day free, 4 runs paid)
  • Ensemble reweighting by recent hit-rate per city/season

7. Risk and gotchas

  • Resolution ambiguity. Weather markets occasionally resolve differently than traders expect because of station choice (JFK vs LGA vs Central Park for NYC). Read the resolution source carefully before every market.
  • Tail risk on black-swan weather. Fronts, heat domes, and polar vortex events blow up ensembles. Fractional Kelly, always.
  • Slippage on thin buckets. Most weather buckets trade <$5k/day volume. Size must respect depth.
  • Polymarket fees and gas. Each order is an on-chain transaction (Polygon). At $1 position sizes, gas becomes a real cost — batch orders or use the gasless CLOB endpoint.
  • Model lag. The edge exists because retail is slow to react to new model runs. If the edge closes (e.g., everyone builds bots), it decays. As of early 2026 it is still live.

Mission. Test whether a calibrated multi-model ensemble forecast of the exact resolution station beats Polymarket's prices on daily temperature and precipitation buckets after fees and fill costs. Running this produces a point-in-time forecast archive, a probability engine scored with Brier and reliability against both the market and climatology, a model-run lag study, a backtest with a control, and a read-only paper trader. 1 · Deeper, not bigger

Before running anything, make the task deeper rather than larger. Ask: what would a top professional do here that I am not asking for; what is the rawest material; which methods and tools; what, other than the model or me, proves it right. Then ask for exactly that.

  • What a top professional would do (that you didn't ask for): Forecast the resolution station, not the city: read each market's rules, identify the exact station and data product it resolves on (often an airport ASOS station as reported by a specific website), and build a per-station bias correction. Score the ensemble with proper scoring rules, Brier score and CRPS plus reliability diagrams, against two baselines: the market's own implied probability and climatology. Use only forecasts issued before each price timestamp, which means archiving or retrieving historical model runs rather than using reanalysis. Test the claimed 30-90 minute lag after model runs directly from price histories. Size with fractional Kelly capped hard, because Kelly on a miscalibrated probability is how small edges blow up.
  • Rawest material to start from: - Each market's rules text and resolution source on Polymarket (Gamma API description field).
  • Station observations: NWS daily climate reports (CLI) and ASOS data via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive for the exact station ids.
  • Open-Meteo Ensemble API (GFS/GEFS, ECMWF IFS ensemble, ICON-EPS, GEM members) and its Previous Runs or Historical Forecast endpoints for point-in-time archives.
  • ECMWF Open Data, NOAA NOMADS GEFS grib2, NWS API gridpoint and MOS products.
  • Polymarket CLOB prices-history and trades for the weather tokens.
  • suislanchez/polymarket-kalshi-weather-bot and hcharper/polyBot-Weather source for reference.
  • Public wallet histories of gopfan2, neobrother, and Hans323 on Polymarket to check the claimed rules and PnL.
  • Methods and tools: Python with httpx, pandas, xarray and cfgrib if parsing grib2; ensemble member counting with kernel dressing to smooth integer-degree buckets; per-station EMOS or simple linear bias correction fit on a rolling window; isotonic recalibration; Brier score, Brier skill score versus market and climatology, CRPS via properscoring, reliability diagrams; weights for ECMWF 35 / GFS 25 / UKMO 20 / NWS 20 compared against weights fit by minimizing CRPS on the training period; event-study of price changes around 00z/06z/12z/18z run availability times; fractional Kelly at 0.25 with a per-position dollar cap; limit-order fill simulation against the book.
  • What proves it right (other than you or the model): The station's official observation decides every scored outcome, not the model. The market's implied probability is the control forecaster: the model must have a lower Brier score than the market out of sample, with a paired bootstrap CI. Climatology is the floor. A 30-day read-only paper run whose hypothetical fills are reconciled to the public trade tape must beat a control ledger. Public wallet data either confirms or refutes the named traders' claimed rules.

Rewritten prompt — ask for exactly that:

Do not build me a weather bot that trades yet. Prove there is an edge against Polymarket prices first.

1. Pull every resolved Polymarket weather market for the past 12 months. From each market's rules, record the exact resolution station and data source. Pull official observations for those stations from NWS CLI reports or the Iowa Environmental Mesonet.
2. Build a point-in-time forecast archive: for each market and each hour before resolution, the most recent ensemble members from Open-Meteo (GEFS, ECMWF IFS ensemble, ICON-EPS, GEM) that were actually available at that hour. No reanalysis.
3. Compute bucket probabilities from members with per-station bias correction and kernel dressing. Compare the guide's fixed weights (ECMWF 35, GFS 25, UKMO 20, NWS 20) against weights fit on a training period. Score with Brier and reliability versus the market's implied probability and climatology on a held-out period.
4. Test the claimed 30-90 minute price lag after each model run.
5. Backtest the rule buy when model probability minus ask exceeds 0.10 with limit-order fills, fees, 0.25 Kelly, and a $10 cap per position, against a control.
6. Run a read-only paper trader for 30 days.

Gate: no real money until the model's Brier beats the market's out of sample and paper beats the control. Any pilot afterward: $300 total, $10 per position, halt after a 20% drawdown. Never promise returns.

3 · Step-by-step prompts

Step 1 — Ground truth: markets, stations, data feeds

Goal: Know exactly what each market resolves on and which forecasts were available when.

Pull all closed Polymarket weather markets for the past 12 months from the Gamma API. For each, parse the rules text and record: city, variable (daily high, low, precipitation), bucket bounds and units, rounding rule, reporting day boundary (local midnight or otherwise), and the exact resolution source (station id such as an airport ASOS code, and the website or product). Save stations.csv and markets.parquet.

For each station, confirm an official observation archive: NWS CLI daily reports or Iowa Environmental Mesonet ASOS. Pull observations and compute each market's true outcome; compare to the on-chain resolution.

Document the forecast feeds: Open-Meteo Ensemble API model list and member counts, the Previous Runs or Historical Forecast endpoint and how far back it goes, whether UKMO ensemble members are available free, ECMWF Open Data run times, NWS MOS availability. Also read Polymarket fees for weather markets and the Terms of Service jurisdiction list. Write ground_truth.md.

Expect: markets.parquet, stations.csv, observations.parquet, ground_truth.md with feed availability table, fee row, jurisdiction finding, and a resolution-match rate.

Audit before moving on: Resolution match between your observation-derived outcome and the on-chain outcome must be 98% or higher; investigate every mismatch (rounding, day boundary, station). Fail if any market lacks a named station; mark it excluded. Flag any model in the guide's weighting (UKMO, NWS) you cannot obtain point-in-time.

Step 2 — Point-in-time forecast archive

Goal: Reconstruct what every model said at every hour before each market resolved.

For every market in markets.parquet, build forecast_archive.parquet: for each hour from 72 hours before resolution to resolution, the most recent run of each model whose publication time is before that hour, with all ensemble members for the station's coordinates and the target variable aggregated over the market's reporting day (daily max of hourly 2 m temperature, or daily precipitation sum). Use Open-Meteo Previous Runs or Historical Forecast endpoints for GEFS, ECMWF IFS ensemble, ICON-EPS, and GEM; add NWS MOS where archived.

Record run initialization time and an assumed publication delay per model (for example GFS available about 3.5-5 hours after init; verify from NOMADS timestamps or Open-Meteo update times). Cache raw API responses by request hash so reruns are free.

Expect: forecast_archive.parquet keyed by market, hour, model, run time, member, value; a coverage table by model and month; raw response cache.

Audit before moving on: For 10 random rows, confirm the run's publication time precedes the archive hour; any violation is look-ahead and fails the step. Check daily-max aggregation uses the market's local reporting day, not UTC. Report member counts per model; a model with zero members for a month is a gap, not a zero.

Step 3 — Probability engine and scoring versus the market

Goal: Turn members into calibrated bucket probabilities and test them against the order book.

Implement bucket probabilities: per model, apply a per-station linear bias correction fit on the training months; kernel-dress members with a Gaussian of width fit to minimize CRPS on training; integrate over each bucket with the rules' rounding. Combine models two ways: the guide's fixed weights (ECMWF 35, GFS 25, UKMO 20, NWS 20, renormalized over available models) and weights fit by minimizing CRPS on training. Add isotonic recalibration on training.

On held-out months only, at lead times 48 h, 24 h, 12 h, and 3 h before resolution, compute Brier score for: fixed-weight ensemble, fitted ensemble, the market's mid-price as a probability, and climatology (station's historical frequency for that bucket and date window). Compute Brier skill scores with paired bootstrap 95% CIs, reliability diagrams, and results by city. Test the guide's 70-75% bucket accuracy claim by stating the top-bucket hit rate.

Expect: scores.md with a table (forecaster x lead time: Brier, BSS vs market, BSS vs climatology, CI), reliability plots, per-city breakdown, top-bucket hit rate.

Audit before moving on: Confirm no parameter was fit on held-out months. If the market's Brier is equal or better than the ensemble at the lead times you would trade, the gate is already failing; say so rather than searching subsets. Count how many city x lead cells you examined before naming winners.

Step 4 — Lag study and backtest with control

Goal: Check the model-run lag claim and simulate trading with real costs.

Lag study: for each model run's publication time, measure the change in model probability and the change in market mid-price over 0-30, 30-90, and 90-180 minutes afterward; compare with matched windows with no new run. Report whether the market moves toward the model with delay and how much of the gap remains after spread.

Backtest on held-out months: entry when fitted-ensemble probability minus best ask is at least 0.10 (and the NO-side mirror); limit orders at or inside the touch that fill only if subsequent trades cross your price; fees from ground_truth.md; 0.25 Kelly with a $10 cap per position and $300 strategy cap; variant with neobrother-style laddering across 3 adjacent buckets. Controls: the gopfan2 price rule (buy YES under 0.15, NO over 0.45, $1 per position) and random entry at matched exposure. Report per arm: trades, fill rate, net PnL, PnL per dollar risked with bootstrap CI, max drawdown, worst day.

Expect: lag_study.md with the event-study table; backtest.md with model strategy, laddered variant, and both controls on held-out data.

Audit before moving on: Rerun the backtest assuming fills only at the far side of the spread; if the result flips, the edge is the spread, not the forecast. Verify that no trade uses a forecast published after its timestamp. Fail if the strategy does not beat both controls with CI excluding zero.

Step 5 — Read-only paper trader

Goal: Gather 30 days of forward evidence without risking money.

Build paper_trader/ in Python: every hour, list open weather markets from the Gamma API, map each to its station via stations.csv, pull the latest available ensemble members from Open-Meteo, compute calibrated bucket probabilities with the frozen parameters from step 3, read the CLOB book, and place hypothetical limit orders by the frozen step 4 rule. A hypothetical fill counts only if real trades later cross the price. Run the gopfan2-rule control and a random-entry control in parallel ledgers.

Log every decision with model probability, market prices, run times used, and order details to paper_ledger.parquet. On resolution, pull the official observation and score. Write a daily summary: open exposure, fills, PnL per ledger, running Brier for model and market. No private key loaded. Run under a systemd user timer with a heartbeat file and alert on staleness.

Expect: paper_trader/ code with README, running timer, paper_ledger.parquet growing, daily summaries.

Audit before moving on: Grep for any signing or key-loading call; any hit fails the step. Reconcile 10 hypothetical fills against the public trade tape. Confirm frozen parameters are hashed and unchanged during the 30 days; a mid-run change restarts the clock.

Step 6 — Validate against oracles, gate, gap report

Goal: Decide go or no-go with evidence and list what remains open.

Evaluate the gate, all must pass: (1) on held-out months, the fitted ensemble's Brier beats the market's implied probability at the traded lead time, paired bootstrap 95% CI excluding zero; (2) it beats climatology; (3) the held-out backtest beats both controls after fees with the far-side-fill assumption; (4) 30 days of paper trading with 100+ reconciled fills beat both paper controls and the running Brier still beats the market; (5) ToS permits the jurisdiction; (6) an independent rerun of scores.md from the cached archive reproduces the Brier numbers.

If all pass, write the pilot spec: $300 total, $10 per position, halt at 20% drawdown or 10 consecutive losing days, daily reconciliation, kill switch. If any fail, verdict is no-go. Write gap_report.md: every guide claim (70-75% accuracy, the four weights, 30-90 minute lag, named trader rules and PnL, sensor myth) and every result, marked verified / unverified / false with evidence, ranked by impact on capital at risk. Fix the top three.

Expect: gate.md with six rows and evidence, one-line verdict, pilot spec only if passed, gap_report.md ranked.

Audit before moving on: Any unverified oracle makes the verdict no-go. Check the report contains no return projection. If a pilot is proposed, confirm caps exist as code constants in the trader.

  • Does my ensemble beat the market's own probability, or only climatology?
  • Are UKMO and NWS actually in my archive, or am I running a two-model ensemble labeled as four?
  • Does the market really lag model runs by 30-90 minutes, and is the residual gap larger than the spread?
  • How much depth sits at the prices my limit orders need, especially on tail buckets?
  • Do gopfan2's and neobrother's public wallets match the rules the guide attributes to them?
  • Is my city or season filter chosen before or after seeing results?
  • What does 0.25 Kelly do to drawdown if my probabilities are 5 points overconfident?
  • Is live trading permitted under the Terms of Service in my jurisdiction?

5 · What proves it right

  • Official station observations (NWS CLI or Iowa Environmental Mesonet ASOS) decide every outcome, matching on-chain resolution in 98%+ of markets.
  • Held-out Brier score of the fitted ensemble beats the market's implied probability at the traded lead time, paired bootstrap 95% CI excluding zero, and beats climatology.
  • Held-out backtest beats both controls (gopfan2 price rule and random entry) after fees under far-side fills.
  • Thirty-day read-only paper run with 100+ fills reconciled to the public trade tape beats both paper controls.
  • Independent recomputation of scores.md from the cached forecast archive reproduces the Brier numbers.

6 · Failure modes

  • Wrong station or product. Tell: resolution-match rate under 98%, misses cluster in one city. Fix: re-read rules, switch to the named station and source.
  • Look-ahead in the forecast archive. Tell: suspiciously high skill at short leads; run publication times after price timestamps. Fix: enforce publication delay per model and rebuild.
  • Beating climatology but not the market. Tell: BSS versus market near zero. Fix: no-go; the order book already knows the forecast.
  • Spread eats the edge. Tell: backtest positive at mid, negative at far-side fills. Fix: trade only where edge minus spread clears the threshold or stop.
  • Overconfident probabilities with Kelly sizing. Tell: reliability curve below the diagonal at high probabilities. Fix: isotonic recalibration and 0.25 Kelly with hard dollar caps.
  • Model unavailable but weighted. Tell: UKMO weight applied to an empty member set. Fix: renormalize over available models and say so.
  • Daily boundary mismatch. Tell: misses on days with late-night temperature peaks. Fix: aggregate over the rules' exact local reporting window.

7 · Agent direction (unattended runs)

Start with rules text and stations: most failures here are resolution mismatches, so step 1 is the most valuable. Cache every API response under data/polymarket_weather/cache/ keyed by request hash and never refetch. Spawn a sub-agent to build the forecast archive (long, rate-limited, self-contained) and a separate one to independently recompute scores as the reproduction oracle. Use Open-Meteo before grib2 parsing; drop to NOMADS or ECMWF Open Data only to fill gaps. Never load or print a private key; the paper trader is read-only. Stop and ask Victor if ToS excludes his jurisdiction, if the held-out sample is under 500 buckets, or before anything that would sign an order. Write outputs to docs/polymarket_weather/: ground_truth.md, scores.md, lag_study.md, backtest.md, gate.md, gap_report.md, and paper_trader/.
