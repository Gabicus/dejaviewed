50k ft — Why care?

Architectural historian James Stevens Curl argues sculpture and decoration were integral to architecture from Greece through Gothic, Renaissance, Rundbogenstil and Egyptian Revival, until modernism removed ornament entirely — so art now feels bolted on as an afterthought.

Transcript excerpt
The sculpture, the decoration, should be absolutely fully integrated with the architecture as it was in ancient Greece, in Roman architecture, in Romanesque architecture, in Gothic architecture, in Renaissance architecture, in Rundbogen steel, in Egyptian revival architecture, and then suddenly the modern movement threw it all out. For the first time in history, anywhere no ornament. And that's why it's so awkward now to have art, because you sort of have to bolt it on as an afterthought. And it doesn't work.
