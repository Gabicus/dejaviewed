Transcript excerpt
One surrealist method I really love is decalcomania. Decalcomania is where our word decal comes from. It's the transfer of a design or a pattern from one surface to the other. And the surrealist version of decalcomania, Oscar Dominguez called it decalcomania with no preconceived object. So without the preconceived design, you let the process itself create random unique textures. So I start with a silhouette of sort of a portrait pose. So I do have some idea of what direction I'm going in, but I have no idea where I'll end up. And I love this technique because it does add an element of uncontrollable chance to it. I can't control how the paint will transfer. I know what the colors will be, but I don't know how it will look. And however it looks after the decalcomania, I like to commit to that. Even while I define it and try and add form and dimension and make it more representational, I want all that stuff to come out of what I see as already being in there. I try to think of it as bringing these random splotches of paint to life.
