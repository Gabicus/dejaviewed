1k ft — Deep-dive / try it

AMD CEO Lisa Su recently demonstrated a compact AI PC powered by AMD’s Ryzen AI Max+ 395, but the most important part was not raw speed. It was memory. Ryzen AI Halo systems can support up to 128GB of unified memory, allowing the CPU and GPU to share one large memory pool. That gives demanding AI models far more room to run locally than they would have on most consumer computers. This matters because large models need more than powerful processors. They also need enough memory to load, operate, and handle complex tasks without relying on cloud servers. Until now, many developers, researchers, and founders have depended on rented GPUs, API credits, hosted tools, and expensive monthly subscriptions.
