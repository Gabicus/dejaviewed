The CNBC fight that cracked Wall Street on live TV. On March 31, 2014, Brad Katsuyama went on CNBC and publicly accused the market of being rigged — sitting feet away from William O’Brien, a senior executive at BATS Global Markets. The backdrop wasn’t drama — it was data. At the time, high-frequency firms were responsible for 50–60% of all U.S. equity trading volume, using speed advantages measured in microseconds to detect and trade ahead of slower orders. Katsuyama claimed exchanges were selling: • Proprietary data feeds • Co-location (servers inside exchange data centers) • Complex order types ...creating a two-tier market: one for institutions and one for machines.
