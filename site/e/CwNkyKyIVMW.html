50k ft — Why care?

Profile of German architect-engineer Frei Otto (1925-2015), Pritzker 2015 winner and founder of Stuttgart's Institute for Lightweight Structures. He form-found membrane and cable-net roofs from soap films, bird skulls and spider webs; works include the 1972 Munich Olympic Stadium and the 1967 Montreal Expo pavilion.

10k ft — Key takeaways

• Otto's experiments are the ancestor of today's parametric architecture. • Physical form-finding (soap films, hanging chains) yields minimal-material structures. • Study the Munich Olympic Stadium roof as the canonical cable-net example.

1k ft — Deep-dive / try it

Pioneer Architect of the Week Frei Otto (1925 - 2015) Frei Paul Otto was a German architect and structural engineer known for his use of lightweight structures, in particular tensile and membrane structures. He won the RIBA Royal Gold Medal in 2006 and the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2015. He founded the Institute for Lightweight Structures at the University of Stuttgart and was head of the department from 1964 to his retirement. Otto tried new methods to reduce material and energy use to create space and experimented with natural objects such as soap bubbles, bird skulls, and spider webs. 🔗 Tap the link in our bio to read the full article.
