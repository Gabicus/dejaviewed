1k ft — Deep-dive / try it

Ozymandias, written by Percy Bysshe Shelley + read by Werner Herzog. “The Arc of Oblivion” is an exploration of archives all around us, in all forms. All around us, nature is creating records. We’re building them each day, from constructing monuments to the photos we take on our phones. It leaves a central question: Why do we do this, knowing they will all fade? Stream the film now on @appletv and @primevideo.
Transcript excerpt
I met a traveller from an antique land who said, Two vast and trunkless legs of stone stand in the desert, Near them on the sand half sunk a shattered visage lies, Whose frown and wrinkled lip and sneer of cold command Tell that it sculpted well those passions red which yet survive, Stamped on these lifeless things the hand that mocked them And the heart that fed, and on the pedestal these words appear, My name is Ozymandias, king of kings, Look at my works, ye mighty and despair, Nothing beside remains, round the decay of that colossal wreck, Boundless and bare, the lone and level sands stretch far away. It can't get any better. That is poetry.
