50k ft — Why care?

Michael Fogleman's ln (Go) renders 3D scenes as 2D vector paths by ray-testing each path segment for visibility, supporting CSG, math surfaces and procedural cities. vpype then merges and sorts paths for plotting, cutting pen-up travel 86% in this example. Both MIT licensed, output straight to SVG.

Transcript excerpt
Today's open source project is LN, the 3D lineart engine. These scenes are 3D, but the output isn't pixels, it's lines. It's by Michael Fogelman, written in Go. He made it so he could plot 3D drawings with his pen plotter. An OpenGL pipeline gives you a raster image. LN gives you 2D vector paths. Every shape is covered in 3D paths. LN chops them into tiny pieces, and from each point shoots a ray toward the camera. If nothing is hit, the point is visible. If something is in the way, it's hidden and never drawn. So the texture is just the paths you choose. Over-eyed paths and a cube become stripes. It also does solid geometry. A sphere trimmed by a cube and drilled by three cylinders. Math functions become surfaces, random walks become strings of beads, and random boxes become a city. Before plotting, there's V-pipe by Antoine Baylor, a Swiss army knife for plotter graphics. Here, line merge and line sort cut the pen up travel by 86%. For visual work, render a scene straight to SVG. Plot it in real ink, or turn a moving camera into a line animation. Both are MIT licensed. Thank you. Bye.
