Zelda, Tears of the Kingdom, and Skyward Sword, plus Mario Kart 8 just got turned into real apps, so you can play them on your Mac, your iPhone, and even a Steam Deck. And the way they pulled it off is the crazy part. Let me explain. Normally, to play a Switch game on a computer, you'd use an emulator. That's an app that pretends to be a Switch and translates the game the whole time you're playing. These translate the game once, ahead of time, into a normal app, so your computer just runs it. Tears of the Kingdom runs on Apple Silicon Macs, and on a Mac Air, it hits around 90 frames a second. You can even stretch it out to ultra-wide, which is something you can't do on a Switch. And it now runs on an iPhone 14 or newer, with touch controls laid out just like a Switch controller. Players even share a list of the game's shaders, the little pieces that draw every effect, so each new build shudders less for everyone. That list is already almost 27,000, and nothing gets sent unless you choose to. Skyward Sword HD now runs natively on Windows, Linux, the Steam Deck, and Mac, at 60 frames per second, up to 4K or ultra-wide. It even has a mod manager built in, so you can turn mods on and off with one click. Mario Kart 8 Deluxe now runs natively on an iPhone and a Mac, and you can race online or on the same Wi-Fi with friends, even ones still on a real Switch. And here's my favorite part.
