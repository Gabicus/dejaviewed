Transcript excerpt
Did you know that in the extreme climate of Abu Dhabi, there are two towers that can automatically adjust their façade based on the Sun's position? Designed by Aidas Architects in 2008, they responded to a significant challenge. How do you design a sustainable skyscraper in a country where temperatures can reach 45 degrees Celsius? Inspired by bio mimicry, the architects turned to nature, observing how pupils adjust to light and flowers respond to the Sun. They also turned to the past, studying the mashrabia, a lattice screen that has been used in Islamic architecture for centuries. This screen diffuses sunlight, keeping buildings cool without obstructing the view or natural light. But how do you design a façade that moves with the Sun? The answer? Or a gami? Using computer-controlled screens, the façade unfolds like an umbrella, directly responding to sunlight. Imagine this. Solar rays can heat window surfaces to nearly 90 degrees Celsius or 200 degrees Fahrenheit. But thanks to this design, the screens shield the glass, reducing glare and letting in diffused light. The result? A 50% reduction in air conditioning demands and less need for artificial lighting. A project where past meets the future, tradition intertwines with innovation and architecture comes alive.
